# DriverPass – System Analysis and Design

## Project Overview

DriverPass was a project where I worked on designing a system for a company that helps people prepare for their driving tests. The client wanted a platform where students could schedule driving lessons, access practice tests, and prepare for their exams. The system also needed to help employees and administrators manage students, instructors, and lesson schedules.

## Reflection

### 1. Summary of the DriverPass Project

The main goal of this project was to understand what the client needed and design a system that could meet those needs. I started by identifying the business requirements and then worked on the system design using UML diagrams. This helped me understand how different users would interact with the system and what features would be needed to make the business run smoothly.

### 2. What I Did Well

I think I did a decent job of breaking down the client's requirements and turning them into a system design. Creating UML diagrams was helpful since it organized my ideas and helped me understand how the different parts of the system would work together. It also helped me look at the system from different users' perspectives instead of focusing only on its technical aspects.

### 3. What I Would Improve

If I could go back and revise one part of my work, I would spend more time reviewing my UML diagrams and making sure the relationships and interactions were clearly represented. I would also double-check that every important requirement was reflected in the design. This would make the documents easier to understand and help avoid confusion during implementation.

### 4. Understanding the User's Needs

I started by looking at the problems DriverPass wanted to solve, such as making it easier for students to book lessons and access study materials. I used these requirements to decide what the system should do and how different users would interact with it. Understanding user needs is important because the purpose of software is to solve real problems. Even a well-built system won't be useful if it doesn't meet the needs of the people using it.

### 5. My Approach to Software Design

My approach to software design is to understand the problem before thinking about the implementation. I would start by gathering requirements, identifying the users, and understanding what each user needs from the system. Then, I would use tools such as UML diagrams to visualize the structure and behavior of the system before writing code. In future projects, I would also spend more time validating requirements, considering edge cases, and reviewing the design before moving into development. I think this helps catch problems early and makes the development process more organized.
